THE EXPERIMENT

Restoration has tradeoffs.

This sandbox explores your team's proposed rules. It uses a synthetic map inspired by a river–estuary system, not a surveyed location or hydrodynamic model.

Your three decisions

Select any levee tile or use the three site buttons to preview an opening. The preview compares six years with and without that opening, assuming no further actions. Confirming spends one token permanently. You can also wait; you never have to use all three.

Playback pauses at years 3, 6, 10, 15, and 20 to let you reconsider. These are game checkpoints, not ecological thresholds. Wildlife emoji represent habitat patches. Select a tile to see its exact status and timers.

What happens each year

  1. Freshwater reaches up to 10 connected tiles from an open breach. Levees and developed tiles block spread.
  2. Within 5 connected tiles, water collects sediment for 3 consecutive years, then becomes mud. Mud needs 3 further connected years to become marsh.
  3. Existing marsh with 3 or more water neighbors erodes when enabled. Five consecutive salty years also kills marsh.
  4. Suitable habitat is occupied immediately. Unsuitable habitat loses its species marker after 2 years. Charts include that grace period.

Changes are simultaneous. River and breach channels stay open. Erosion uses 4 orthogonal neighbors; distance uses grid steps through the connected basin. Developed tiles are barriers, with no flood damage model.

Read the evidence

Freshwater diversion can change salinity and oyster growing conditions, but these tile thresholds and timelines are invented for gameplay.
